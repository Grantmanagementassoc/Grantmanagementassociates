Federal Research — NSF Restructured Solicitation Suite Between August 17 and August 19, NSF posted twelve directorate-level research solicitations across MPS, ENG, BIO, CISE, and GEO. Read together they represent a structural change, not a routine reissue: NSF has consolidated large numbers of legacy single-program announcements into a small number of broad, directorate-level solicitations, most of which accept proposals at any time with no deadline. The recurring language across the set — that NSF is "particularly interested in intellectually ambitious and potentially transformative foundational research" and that proposals "need not promise a near-term product, deployment, or commercial outcome" — is a stated shift away from translational framing and toward foundational work. For university and research-institute clients, the practical consequences are that the legacy program page a PI has bookmarked may no longer be the operative solicitation, and that the absence of a deadline shifts submission timing from a calendar problem to a strategic one. Policy Watch California — CEC Approves $95.2 Million Clean Transportation Program Investment Plan Allocation California Energy Commission — $95.2 Million ZEV Infrastructure Allocation (Advance Notice) Document Governor’s Office announcement, "Governor Newsom announces $95 million plan to expand EV charging and hydrogen fueling across California," published August 18, 2026, reporting a California Energy Commission action taken Monday, August 17, 2026. What It Is An allocation decision, not a solicitation. The CEC approved how its Clean Transportation Program investment plan will deploy $95.2 million in the current fiscal year, with projected allocations running through 2028-2029. No application is open. Individual solicitations will follow from the CEC against these categories. Allocation Breakdown $48,000,000 — light-duty EV charging infrastructure, with emphasis on DC fast charging and at-home or near-home charging. $30,200,000 — medium- and heavy-duty ZEV infrastructure, with emphasis on freight, port, public fleet, and school bus applications. $15,000,000 — hydrogen refueling. $2,000,000 — ZEV workforce training and development. The Requirement That Shapes Scoping At least 50% of program funds must go to projects that benefit or serve low-income Californians and residents of low-income and disadvantaged communities. As of March 2026 more than 62% of Clean Transportation Program and supplemental funds had gone to projects in disadvantaged or low-income communities, so the statutory floor understates the practical competitive standard. A project sited outside a designated community will be competing against a field in which the majority of awards historically go inside one. Program Context The Clean Transportation Program has invested more than $2.7 billion since 2008. Cumulative results reported: more than 52,000 EV chargers installed or planned, including roughly 41,000 lower-power chargers and more than 11,000 DC fast chargers; more than 40 manufacturing projects; more than 30 workforce training projects; and the nation’s largest hydrogen refueling network at over 100 stations. California now has 216,445 publicly available and shared EV charging ports and has surpassed 20,000 DC fast chargers.
