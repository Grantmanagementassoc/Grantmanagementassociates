GMA Note

The applicable client profile here is narrow but specific: a statewide or regional food bank association, or a national intermediary with demonstrated subaward administration capacity. A single food bank seeking a walk-in cooler is not the applicant — it is the intended subrecipient. Any client considering this should be tested first against 2 CFR 200.332 pass-through entity obligations: subrecipient risk assessment, monitoring, and audit follow-up are the substance of the award, and an organization without existing subaward infrastructure will struggle on the programmatic capability criteria. Equipment acquisitions at the subrecipient level also carry 2 CFR 200.313 property standards — title, use, and disposition rules that survive the project period.

Manufacturing / Industrial Base and Supply Chain

U.S. Small Business Administration — 2026 Critical Suppliers Prize Competition

Opportunity #

None assigned. This is a prize competition authorized under the America COMPETES Act (15 U.S.C. § 3719), not posted on Grants.gov or SAM.gov. Entries are submitted by email to investinnovate@sba.gov; there is no application package and no fee.

Agency / Funder

U.S. Small Business Administration

Total Funding

Up to $20,000,000 in prize awards.

Award Range

SBA anticipates 4 to 6 winners at up to $6,000,000 each. Prizes are paid against projected and completed milestone achievements. One prize per winning submission regardless of how many parties contributed to it.

Eligible Applicants

U.S.-based small businesses meeting SBA size standards, organized in and maintaining a primary place of business in the United States or its territories. One entry per business. All members of the senior management team must be U.S. citizens or permanent residents over 18.

The financial screens are the real gate: contestants must currently be profitable, demonstrate general creditworthiness, be current and in good standing on all federal obligations, and be in good standing in their state of organization. Any prior default on a federal loan or federally guaranteed financing that caused the government a loss is disqualifying — and the bar reaches through to owners who previously owned, operated, or controlled a business that defaulted. A compromise agreement under 31 C.F.R. 3711 counts as a loss for this purpose.

Ineligible: SBA employees and contractors, federal entities and employees acting within the scope of employment, and any party currently suspended or debarred.

Deadline

August 28, 2026, 11:59 PM Eastern, by email to investinnovate@sba.gov. SBA intends to announce winners on or about September 10, 2026.

Program Focus

Non-dilutive prize capital to expand production capacity at identified critical supply chain chokepoints. Submissions must deliver tangible results in at least one of three priority areas:

Advanced Metals Manufacturing — rapid tooling, precision casting and forging, heat treated components, strategic and critical minerals, and rare earth element recovery and magnet production.

Advanced Materials Manufacturing — large format additive manufacturing and materials transformation.

Energy Systems, Energetics and Components — nuclear energy, battery energy storage systems, and standardization efforts.

The stated purpose is to refine, accelerate, and scale existing solutions rather than to fund research and development. Contestants must be able to demonstrate a measurable production increase or a production efficiency gain within six months of award, and must show the ability to deploy funds immediately. SBA suggests submissions take the form of a company pitch deck covering business description, operating history and current capital needs, near-term milestone and use of proceeds, revenue forecast with and without the award, and references. Judging weighs management character and cohesion, business model, execution certainty, and mission alignment. SBA logo and seal use is prohibited in submissions.

Funding Instrument

Prize payment under 15 U.S.C. 3719 — not a grant, cooperative agreement, or contract. Intellectual property arising from the competition remains with the contestant. Awards carry no Uniform Guidance award terms and no indirect cost recovery.

Cost Share / Match

No match required, but the structure functions like one in practice: award proceeds may not be used for general overhead (SG&A), and the submission must identify other sources of capital deployed alongside the prize funds.

Primary Sector

Defense Industrial Base; Advanced Manufacturing; Critical Minerals and Rare Earths; Additive Manufacturing; Nuclear Energy; Battery Energy Storage; Supply Chain Resilience; Reshoring

GMA Note

Seven days out from an email-only close, so this is a triage call, not a build. The screening question is short: is the client profitable today, clean on federal credit history, and sitting on a scoped capacity expansion it could start inside six months? If any of those three is no, the answer is no — the eligibility criteria are stated as absolute conditions rather than scoring factors, and SBA reserves the right to re-vet at any point in the process.

Federal Research — NSF Restructured Solicitation Suite

Between August 17 and August 19, NSF posted twelve directorate-level research solicitations across MPS, ENG, BIO, CISE, and GEO. Read together they represent a structural change, not a routine reissue: NSF has consolidated large numbers of legacy single-program announcements into a small number of broad, directorate-level solicitations, most of which accept proposals at any time with no deadline. The recurring language across the set — that NSF is "particularly interested in intellectually ambitious and potentially transformative foundational research" and that proposals "need not promise a near-term product, deployment, or commercial outcome" — is a stated shift away from translational framing and toward foundational work. For university and research-institute clients, the practical consequences are that the legacy program page a PI has bookmarked may no longer be the operative solicitation, and that the absence of a deadline shifts submission timing from a calendar problem to a strategic one.

California — CEC Approves $95.2 Million Clean Transportation Program Investment Plan Allocation

California Energy Commission — $95.2 Million ZEV Infrastructure Allocation (Advance Notice)

Document

Governor’s Office announcement, "Governor Newsom announces $95 million plan to expand EV charging and hydrogen fueling across California," published August 18, 2026, reporting a California Energy Commission action taken Monday, August 17, 2026.

What It Is

An allocation decision, not a solicitation. The CEC approved how its Clean Transportation Program investment plan will deploy $95.2 million in the current fiscal year, with projected allocations running through 2028-2029. No application is open. Individual solicitations will follow from the CEC against these categories.

Allocation Breakdown

$48,000,000 — light-duty EV charging infrastructure, with emphasis on DC fast charging and at-home or near-home charging.

$30,200,000 — medium- and heavy-duty ZEV infrastructure, with emphasis on freight, port, public fleet, and school bus applications.

$15,000,000 — hydrogen refueling.

$2,000,000 — ZEV workforce training and development.

The Requirement That Shapes Scoping

At least 50% of program funds must go to projects that benefit or serve low-income Californians and residents of low-income and disadvantaged communities. As of March 2026 more than 62% of Clean Transportation Program and supplemental funds had gone to projects in disadvantaged or low-income communities, so the statutory floor understates the practical competitive standard. A project sited outside a designated community will be competing against a field in which the majority of awards historically go inside one.

Program Context

The Clean Transportation Program has invested more than $2.7 billion since 2008. Cumulative results reported: more than 52,000 EV chargers installed or planned, including roughly 41,000 lower-power chargers and more than 11,000 DC fast chargers; more than 40 manufacturing projects; more than 30 workforce training projects; and the nation’s largest hydrogen refueling network at over 100 stations. California now has 216,445 publicly available and shared EV charging ports and has surpassed 20,000 DC fast chargers.
