Agriculture / Specialty Crop Export Technology

USDA Foreign Agricultural Service — Assisting Specialty Crop Exports: Low Oxygen Storage and Packaging Systems for U.S. Tree Nuts with Phytosanitary Traceability

Opportunity #

USDA-FAS-10621-0750-106-27-0001; Assistance Listing 10.621 (Assisting Specialty Crop Exports). Grants.gov opportunity 363690.

Agency / Funder

U.S. Department of Agriculture — Foreign Agricultural Service

Total Funding

$2,000,000.

Award Range

$2,000,000 maximum; one award anticipated at the full program amount. There is no partial-award path.

Eligible Applicants

This opportunity is available to U.S. academic institutions, state governments, nonprofits with or without 501(c)(3) designation, U.S. trade associations, U.S. agricultural cooperatives, international organizations, foreign entities, and any other entity including private sector businesses.

Deadline

October 26, 2026.

Program Focus

Tree nut handlers increasingly use modified or controlled atmosphere storage holding oxygen below 1% by volume. Low-oxygen conditions control insect pests, leave no chemical residue, and suppress the lipid oxidation that causes rancidity — but there is currently no efficient way for a shipper to validate those conditions to a customer, let alone to an importing government with phytosanitary requirements.

Existing low-oxygen technologies do not incorporate digital mapping or official record-keeping during packing and storage, so there is no non-invasive method to verify oxygen content over time inside gas-impermeable liners or to confirm seal integrity. The program funds development of technically and economically viable, verifiable, and preferably traceable technology to close that gap.

The trade context is concrete: India, Korea, and Japan require phytosanitary treatment such as fumigation for post-harvest pest management before many U.S. tree nuts may be imported, and buyers in other markets request treatment as a condition of sale. Fumigation and freezing are both expensive and create marketing bottlenecks.

Funding Instrument

Cooperative agreement.

Cost Share / Match

No match required.

Primary Sector

Tree Nuts (almond, walnut, pistachio); Post-Harvest Technology; Controlled and Modified Atmosphere Storage; Phytosanitary Compliance and Traceability; Agricultural Export Market Access; Sensor and Digital Monitoring Systems

U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service — Cooperative Endangered Species Conservation Fund: Habitat Conservation Plan Land Acquisition Grants

Opportunity #

F26AS00071; Assistance Listing 15.615 (Cooperative Endangered Species Conservation Fund). Grants.gov opportunity 363691.

Agency / Funder

U.S. Department of the Interior — Fish and Wildlife Service

Total Funding

$26,000,000.

Award Range

$10,000 minimum to $26,000,000 maximum; approximately 10 awards anticipated.

Eligible Applicants

State and territorial agencies only, and only those that have entered into a cooperative agreement under section 6(c) of the Endangered Species Act and have supplied the information needed for annual renewal of that agreement.

Counties, conservation organizations, and land trusts participate as subgrantees working with an eligible State agency — they cannot apply directly. In California that means the California Department of Fish and Wildlife is the applicant of record for any project a local partner wants funded.

Deadline

October 30, 2026, 11:59 PM Eastern, through GrantSolutions.gov.

Program Focus

Created by Congress in FY 1997 to reduce conflict between conservation of listed species and competing land uses on specific parcels associated with approved and permitted Habitat Conservation Plans. FWS provides matching grants to State agencies for land acquisition that conserves species habitat in perpetuity, through fee simple purchase or permanent conservation easement.

Land proposed for acquisition must benefit at least one federally listed species included on an active section 10(a)(1)(B) permit. Parcels must be individually identified on the SF-429 and SF-429-B to be eligible, though they may be grouped at the property or transaction level for evaluation. Where specific properties are not yet identified, an applicant may describe an area of similar habitat type supporting a similar suite of species with an estimated acreage, plus the procedure that will ensure high-quality habitat is targeted.

Applications must include a commitment to fund and implement management of the habitat in perpetuity consistent with the conservation needs of the species. A property may not be submitted under both the HCP Land Acquisition and Recovery Land Acquisition programs in the same fiscal year, and applications cannot be submitted to cover administrative costs alone.

Participation is entirely voluntary. Eligible properties are working forest and rangelands managed for production of forest products where traditional forest uses are maintained. A landowner may sell or transfer particular rights — development rights, public access — while retaining ownership and any use consistent with the easement and management plan.

Community Forests require full fee title acquisition and may be owned by local governments, Tribal governments, or qualified nonprofits. Public access is required and the land must be managed under a community forest plan.

Deadline

Status by track, as published. Note 2026 Forest Legacy Program Capacity and Project Development Grant Solicitation is scheduled to open Sept. 8 but award amounts are only $50K-$150K.

Program Focus

Protects environmentally important forest land threatened with conversion to non-forest uses, so that it continues to provide sustainable timber production, wildlife habitat, recreation, watershed protection, open space, and carbon storage and sequestration.

CAL FIRE purchases or accepts donations of conservation easements or fee title on productive forest lands. The primary tool is the permanent Working Forest Conservation Easement, which goes beyond restricting development and conversion by concentrating on sustainable forest practices that produce economic value from the land and encourage long-term stewardship.

Projects are evaluated on four characteristics: Importance (economic, habitat, watershed, water supply, recreational, scenic, and historical or cultural benefits); Strategic Value (relationship to adjacent or nearby protected properties); Threat (exposure to development or conversion); and Readiness (extent of completed due diligence). Submitted parcels require a field review by CAL FIRE and may require review by the USDA Forest Service, the State Forest Stewardship Coordinating Committee, or other agencies.

Funding Instrument

Grant funding for acquisition of conservation easements or fee title.

Cost Share / Match

Varies by track. The Community Forest Program requires a 50% non-federal match. Match terms for the state and federal Forest Legacy tracks are set in the applicable solicitation.
